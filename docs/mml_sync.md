# MML synchronization points

Japanese original: [mml_syncJ.md](mml_syncJ.md).

The combined file `<stem>.mml` generated by `vgm2mml.py` joins the MML for all sound chips,
then calculates each channel's cumulative steps and displays common synchronization points as comments.
No postprocessing script or additional options are required.

To avoid excessively dense comments, the default selects the first common synchronization point
at least 1000 steps after the previously selected point that does not split a loop or macro.
The start at 0 and the end of the song are displayed regardless of spacing.
For example, if common boundaries outside loops occur every 12 steps, the points are 0 → 1008 → 2016 → ….
Notes are never cut at exactly 1000 steps.
Use `--sync-min-gap 700` to change the spacing, or `--sync-min-gap 0` to display all synchronization points
that preserve loops and macros. This controls comment density; it does not detect phrases or measures.

```mml
; ch1 --- step 0 : start ---
1 c%6 d%6
; ch1 --- step 12 : end ---

; ch4 --- step 0 : start ---
4 g%12
; ch4 --- step 12 : end ---
```

Intermediate synchronization points use the form `; ch1 --- step 48 : token-boundary 7/7ch ---`.
Only positions where note or rest boundaries align across all channels still playing at that time are selected.
Channels that have already finished, and setup-only channels with no durations, are excluded from subsequent checks.
The start is 0, and the final point is the ending step of the longest channel. No rests are appended to finished channels.
No synchronization point is placed inside a chain of tied notes.

A step is the output MML time unit: a quarter note is 48 steps. It is neither a VGM sample count nor a Segment tick count.
Normal output and `--raw-ticks` express tempo and note lengths differently, so their step values differ even for the same VGM.
Note/rest lengths and sound-chip analysis are unchanged.

The interiors of repeats and macro calls are excluded from synchronization candidates, preserving the original structure.
Repeats containing no synchronization point are retained.
The combined MML distributes the total `#alloc` of 15000 in proportion to the MML character count
of each output performance channel (excluding whitespace, comments, and channel numbers at line starts).
Remainders after integer conversion are distributed in descending order of fractional part, bringing the total to 15000.
Channels containing only rests, or whose notes all have volume 0, are excluded from both the combined MML body and synchronization checks.
This is an estimated usage amount, not a measured byte count after compilation.
There are also ordinary wrapped lines without comments; not every line break is a synchronization point.

The implementation is separated into `py/mml_sync.py`. It handles melodic MML generated by this converter;
it is not a general-purpose MGSDRV parser. Unsupported syntax and infinite loops cause errors rather than being guessed.
Per-chip MML, event CSVs, PASS outputs, and Segments remain available through `--debug --dump-passes`.

Validation expands repeats and macros before and after comment insertion and compares the order of notes and commands,
their starting steps, and their ending steps.
All synchronization candidates in the supplied sample `cpebach_fine_grained.mml` are `0, 4490, 4538, 4778`.
With a minimum gap of 1000, `0, 4490, 4778` are displayed (the last is an exception because it is the end).
MML newly converted from VGM uses the note boundaries created by the existing conversion process,
so its step values do not necessarily match this sample.

## PSG/SCC rests and software envelopes

`py/mml_envelopes.py` assembles sounding intervals for MML without changing Segments.
During rests it emits no volume, mode, waveform, pitch, or similar settings; settings for the next sounding interval
are applied immediately before it. Consecutive rests are combined while preserving their duration and sound onset times.

Volume trajectories are extracted from intervals with constant pitch, waveform, and mode, and converted to reusable `@e` definitions.
Volume increases split intervals as re-attack candidates; frequency writes and PSG hardware-envelope resets also form boundaries.
This is a conservative inference of sounding intervals, not a claim that VGM directly contains note information.
Hardware envelopes are not replaced with software envelopes.

Definitions explicitly hold volume values, for example `@eNN = { 0, 0, A:2, 8:2, 6:2 }`,
and are applied with `v15`. PSG mode and noise are specified separately so that definitions do not change them.
When returning to constant volume, the definition returns to one with a constant F value.
The process does not reverse-engineer interpolation parameters such as `F, E, 8=12` in expected MML;
it preserves the volume observed on each 60 Hz tick.
Envelope time units remain at 60 Hz, separate from the threefold MML note-length representation used in normal output.

Definition numbers 0–31 are shared by PSG/SCC, and identical volume trajectories are not registered twice.
When definition slots are exhausted or a definition is too long, volume commands and tied notes are used instead.
With `--dump-passes`, `*.target_notes.csv` records the channel, start/end ticks, selected definition number,
and original volume trajectory. `*.target.mml` saves the applied definitions and per-chip bodies.
Existing events, PASS outputs, Segments, and the legacy renderer output from `--debug` are also retained.

## Preserving loops and macros

Across all parts, only boundaries of top-level notes, loops, and macro calls are synchronization candidates.
Note boundaries within repeats and boundaries between iterations are not candidates while they remain inside the original loop.
For nested loops, the entire outer loop is preserved; loops are not expanded to insert comments.
Positions inside ties remain excluded as before. Finished channels do not obstruct later candidates.

This applies to PSG, SCC, OPLL melody, and OPLL rhythm. If no safe common boundary exists,
comment spacing may exceed the specified value, and only start/end comments may appear.
Notes, note lengths, repeat counts, and setup commands are unchanged. Physical line wrapping continues.
